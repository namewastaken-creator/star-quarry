## Planets and mining crews
- 22 planets. Claiming a planet means hiring its first mining crew, the Crew Chief. Cinder starts with its Crew Chief free.
- **Fixed handling times (the player's rule, Beta 1.0.77):** nothing may change the 28 s of docking, orbiting, landing and loading (`handleTime` is always `ECON.SHIP_OVH`). Upgrades only speed up the flight between station and planet. Beta 1.0.77 removed the Handling trait, Dock training, the − 2 s perk and the − 2 s quirk; Handling points came back as free points, and a slot that held the − 2 s perk was emptied with a free swap. Pacing unchanged: warp 5 at 18.9 h (19.4 h with parking), all 22 planets at 95.1 h. Beta 1.0.78 fixed a crash in hiring: removing the − 2 s quirk had also dropped the "Training programs 25% faster" quirk that Instructors crews can bring.
- **Crews and the point pool (Beta 1.0.76, replacing specialist roles):** up to **3 crews** per planet. Each crew adds room for 20 upgrade points (`PTS_PER_CREW`), and all of a planet's points go into one pool (`p.pts` owned, `p.bought` bought, `p.al` allocation). Points aren't tracked per crew. Each crew's name and job sit above the area it controls (Beta 1.0.79): the Crew Chief above Points, the Shift Crew above Perks, the Instructors above Training programs (`crewBy`). Beta 1.0.80 dropped the separate crew list: the card header shows "x / 3 Crews hired", and the Shift Crew's and Instructors' quirks show under their names.
  - **Hire costs:** Crew Chief = the planet's claim price; Shift Crew = 8× scale; Instructors = 30× scale (`ECON.HIRE`). Scale is the larger of the claim price and 100.
  - **Hiring order:** the next crew can be hired once the planet owns every point its crews have room for (`hireReady`): 20 for the Shift Crew, 40 for the Instructors. The hire panel shows progress until then, then three candidates.
  - **Point price:** scale × 0.12 × 1.18^(points bought) (`pointCost`, `ECON.PT_GROW`), whichever crew's room it fills; × 0.9 with a 'thrifty' crew. Free points from events don't raise the price.
- **Crew Chief (crew 1):** allocates every point. Mine rate, Speed and Cargo are + 0.25× each (Mine rate + 0.3× after Deep drilling). Ore yield appears once Prospecting is done (`traitOK`, `traitStep`). − takes a point back for free; + places a free point or buys one.
  - **Ore yield:** × (1 + 0.02 × points) on every delivered load, applied after hauling (`YIELD_STEP`).
- **Shift Crew (crew 2):** + 20 points and perk slots (`perkSlots`): 1 when hired, 2 once the planet has 40 points, 3 with Advanced shifts. Perks come from one library (`PERKS`): + 12% mine rate, ship speed or cargo; + 2 silo loads; overflow sells at 50%; lighter hulls; and, after Prospecting, + 8% ore yield, rich veins twice as often or 50% bigger. A perk can be in only one slot. Changing a perk takes a 5-minute refit (`REFIT`, half with a 'fastrefit' crew), during which the slot does nothing; filling an empty slot is instant (`setPerk`).
- **Instructors (crew 3):** + 20 points and training programs (`PROGRAMS`), one at a time, each costing credits (× scale) and time, kept for the rest of the galaxy and finished by the clock, also while away (`startProg`, `progTick`):
  - Prospecting (40×, 45 min): unlocks Ore yield, rich veins (one every hour: 2 minutes of mining straight to the Market) and bigger probe hauls. **Beta 0.19.5.116:** probes no longer favour Prospecting planets' ores (half of launches used to pick from their ores); instead every planet with Prospecting makes all probe hauls 2% bigger, counting up to 5 planets (+10%; `PROS_PROBE`, `PROS_PROBE_MAX`, `prosProbe`, `probeMult` = Settlements × permanent × Prospecting, used by `probeReturn`, the Probe card's Expected haul and Worth about, and `evProbeValRate`). The Probe card's 'Expected haul' now includes all multipliers, including a comet pass. Simulator: removing the targeting alone made the full game 96.3 h; 5% per planet up to 10 made it 84.3 h; 2% up to 5 gave warp 5 at 19.1 h and all 22 planets at 92.5 h (was 94.2 h). **Beta 0.19.6.117:** changed to 1% per planet with no cap (`PROS_PROBE` = .01; `PROS_PROBE_MAX` removed), so up to +22% with all 22 planets: warp 5 at 18.9 h, all 22 planets at 92.3 h. The Probe card reads 'Each planet with Prospecting makes hauls 1% bigger (now +N%)'.
  - Deep drilling (60×, 60 min): Mine rate points + 0.3×.
  - Logistics (30×, 30 min): + 2 silo loads, overflow sells at 40%.
  - Advanced shifts (50×, 60 min): a third perk slot.
  - Mentoring (80×, 120 min): room for 5 more points.
- **Candidates and quirks:** the Shift Crew and the Instructors are picked from three named crews (`crewCands`, seeded from the galaxy's start, planet and slot, so they can't be rerolled). Quirks fit the job (`CREW_QUIRKS`, `QUIRK_POOL`): Shift Crew + 10% mine rate, ship speed or cargo, points 10% cheaper, 2 free points, or faster refits; Instructors training 25% faster or cheaper, points 10% cheaper, 2 free points or + 1 silo load.
- **After a station move:** the planet the station parked at, and planets whose ships carry under 85% of mining afterwards (`PRIO_BELOW`), get one free, instant perk swap until the next move (`swapOffers`, `p.swapFree`). The parking forecast flags them. (This replaced the free retrain and Priority Training.)
- **Old saves:** every crew's points move into the pool, allocated where they were (capped at 20 per crew); Dockhands and Prospectors count as finished Dock training and Prospecting; quirks that suit the crew's new job are kept; old veteran perks are dropped and planets with a Shift Crew get a free perk swap.
- **Best upgrade:** measured. `planetIncome(i)` = (hauled + rich veins) × yield + overflow at its sale price; `pointGain(i,k)` adds a point, measures and takes it back; the red button is the trait with the biggest gain (`bestTrait`).
- **Pacing (Beta 1.0.76):** base output divisor 3.5 (was 3.3), point price growth 1.18. Simulator bot (hires crews when ready, buys the best trait, fills empty perk slots with the best perk, runs the cheapest program when idle): warp 1 at 3.2 h, warp 5 at 18.8 h (18.9 h with parking), all 22 planets at 94.5 h (94.7 h with parking).
- **Card title:** the Planet tab's crews card is titled 'Specialists'. Order: crews (name, job, quirk), hire panel, Points (owned / room, bar, free points or next point's price), tuning chart, trait rows, the 'red button' note, Shift Crew perks, Training programs.
- **Trait rows:** name, points on it (with any planned change), the trait's total multiplier, any Bottleneck tag, and a value line. 98 px rows; + (46 px) and − (27 px) buttons keep one size in every state.
- **Tuning chart (Beta 1.0.59):** the Mining crews card has a chart above the trait rows (`tuneUI`). It shows the mining line (orange), hauling capacity through the next cycle (teal curve), its average (dotted) and red/teal shading, plus a status line and the planet's income. It redraws with every change.
  - **Plans:** point changes per trait on the planet's pool (`tune[i].plan`, `{k: ±n}`, in memory only), tried on a copy of the planet (`withPlan`). Removals are free; placements use free points first, then buy upgrades (`planBuys`, `planCost`). Dashed lines show the result, with the change in words ('+ 4 Cargo · + 1 Speed'), the cost, the income change, and Apply / Cancel (`planApply`). Plans are cleared on hire or retrain and dropped if they stop being valid.
  - **No Preview toggle (removed in Beta 1.0.61):** a drag or Balance creates the plan. While a plan is showing, + and − (labelled 'Plan') change it; with no plan they act at once as before.
  - **Drag handles:** orange (mining) on the left edge, teal (average hauling: Speed and Cargo) on the right edge, 22 px, each with ▲▼ arrows (Beta 1.0.62). Only the handles drag, with a 10 px invisible grab area around each (Beta 1.0.74); touching anywhere else on the chart does nothing, so swiping over it scrolls the panel. Dragging plans the cheapest whole-point change toward the target (`planTo`): free points first, otherwise the best capacity per credit across Speed and Cargo that can take them; dragging down removes the points that lose least. The panel stops redrawing during a drag (`tdrag`); only the chart and summary update, at a fixed scale.
  - **Balance (Beta 1.0.68):** places every free point, then hill-climbs single-point moves between the planet's traits (`planBalance`). Any layout where the ships keep up all cycle (`keepsUp`: overflow ≤ 0.2% of mining) beats any that doesn't; among those it takes the most income, and among the rest the least overflow (`balScore`). It never buys anything. If it can't reach 'keeps up', it offers the cheapest hauling points to buy on top ('To keep up: … · Add', `planKeepUp`: free points first, then most overflow removed per credit), or says the crews need a hire, a retrain or research if they run out of room.
  - **Balance window (Beta 0.17.1.93):** tapping Balance opens a dialog (`openBalance`) instead of a dashed preview. It shows the free rearrangement (`planBalance`) and its income change, then a slider to buy upgrades on top. `blSteps` lists purchases one point at a time from the balanced layout: each step adds the point with the best `balScore`, then up to 3 free moves that help, as far as the player can afford and the crews have room. The slider starts at the most affordable; the window shows the spend (of current credits), the net changes, income now → after, whether ships keep up all cycle, and payback time. Apply buys the chosen step (`planApply`); the left end is the free rearrangement only. If nothing can move and nothing is affordable, a toast gives the next point's price or says to hire the next crew. The old 'To keep up: … · Add' offer is no longer shown.
  - **Honest income model (Beta 1.0.68):** `planetIncome` uses `planetFlow`, which steps through a whole cycle (after a warm-up cycle, 64 steps) with the planet's real silo: what the ships carry, and what overflows (sold at 25%). Before, it assumed an unlimited silo, so Balance and the red button pushed mining past what the silo could hold. When the ships' average capacity is below mining, the run starts with the silo full (Beta 1.0.75): starting empty let a big silo (3 or 8 loads) hide a shortfall of a few percent for the two cycles measured, so Balance pushed mining just past the ships and the planet's name turned red. The chart's status line uses the same model. Pacing: warp 5 at 18.2–18.5 h, all 22 planets at 96.7–98.2 h.
- There are no direct planet upgrades. The Planet tab has two parts:
  - **Mined vs hauled (Beta 1.0.60):** no separate card any more; Mined x/s and Hauled avg x/s head the tuning chart in the Mining crews card.
  - **Mining crews card:** each trait row combines the planet stat with the team upgrade. Mine rate (ore/s), Ship speed (AU/s · trip) and Cargo hold (ore/trip · −6% flight/pt). Each row shows the planet total; the selected team's name and its own multiplier (e.g. 'Flint Crew × 1.50', plus 'Settlement × 1.5' when one applies); a 'Next:' preview of what one more point does (ore/s, trip time, ship capacity = cargo/trip time); and the upgrade button. When ships are behind, Mine rate's preview adds 'income capped at x/s'. Only Ship speed or Cargo hold can carry the Bottleneck tag. Rows are a fixed 118px so nothing jumps.
  - Team buttons for the bottleneck trait are solid red. Buttons you can't press (not enough credits, or maxed) fade to a dark fill with a coloured outline: teal for green buttons, red for red ones. When the selected team is maxed (20/20), its Maxed button for the bottleneck trait shows the faded red look, signalling that the fix has to come from another team or from − / + rebalancing.
- **Fine-tuning with + and −:** each trait row shows the points on it. It has a '+ 0.25' button (place a free point or buy, with Free or the price underneath) and a '− 0.25' button stacked below. − takes one point back into the team's free points at no cost. The rows are titled Speed and Cargo, not Ship speed and Cargo hold, so the count and the Bottleneck tag fit on a 360 px phone. The card's note reads 'The red button is the most efficient upgrade.' Operators in these rows have spaces around them (× 1.50, − 6%, + 0.25).
- The planet name row has ‹ › scroll buttons and edge fades, and centers the selected planet. It is pinned (sticky) to the top of the panel, on a solid background with a shadow, while the page scrolls under it. Switching planets keeps the scroll position: the Planet tab remembers where the player scrolled (`planetScroll`). A planet with a shorter page sits at its bottom without overwriting that, so the next longer page returns to it. Tapping a planet in the sky while the Planet tab is open no longer jumps to the top; opening the tab from another tab still starts at the top. Picking a planet there keeps the current zoom: a close-up moves to the new planet's close-up, a zoomed-out follow moves to it at the same distance, and the galaxy view only changes the selection.

## Settlements
- **Unlock:** a planet with all 3 mining crews can found a settlement, then grow it in 3 levels: Outpost, Town, City.
- **Cost:** 600×, 3,000× and 15,000× the planet's scale (claim price, or 100 minimum).
- **Upgrades:** each planet has its own settlement name and three named upgrades:
  - Level 1: ×1.5 to that planet's mining, ship speed or cargo.
  - Level 2: another local ×1.5, or ×1.5 sale value for the planet's newest ore and its bars.
  - Level 3: a galaxy-wide perk: all mining, ship speed or cargo ×1.1, all sale prices ×1.05, smelting ×1.25 or probe hauls ×1.25.
  - Examples: Cinder's Ashfall (Lava Vents, Magma Foundry, Forge Heart), Mirrow's Frosthold (Thaw Drills, Ice Runners, Aurora Beacons), Zenith's Zenith Throne (Crown Scoops, Halo Docks, Zenith Accord). The full list is `SETTLE` in the game script.
- **Interface:**
  - Once all teams are hired, the top button of the Planet tab founds or grows the settlement.
  - A Settlement card lists the three upgrades, what each does and which are built.
  - Read-outs show the settlement's share (for example "×1.5 settlement").
  - Planet labels show one ◆ per level, on a line above the planet name.
- **Warp:** settlements reset with the planets.
- **Graphics:** the settlement sits south of the launch pad, about 6 colony units from the hall and 8 from the nearest ore field. Twin transit tubes join it to a terminal on the pad's south edge.
  - It has its own surface frame centered on the plaza, with north pointing at the pad, so buildings keep true size and spacing on the curved surface.
  - Buildings are 1.25× base scale. Roofs and trim use the planet's second color.
  - The layout is checked with true surface distances against the pad, its tower and hangar, the domes and the solar panels.
  - Every building is a dome habitat with a lit window band and a trim band in the planet's color, joined by covered walkways.
  - **Link to the pad:** a transit terminal stands on the launch pad's south edge. It has a glass front facing the pad, a door, a canopy in the planet's color, a glowing sign and a lamp.
  - Twin enclosed transit tubes run from two lit portals in the terminal's back wall to the central hub, on supports with teal light rings.
  - **Central hub dome** (grows each level):
    - Outpost: a low dome with a window band and a glowing trim ring.
    - Town: a drum with two window bands rises through it, topped by a second dome and ring.
    - City: a third tier with a glass crown and glowing core, a turning halo with lit pods, an antenna, a pulsing beacon, and ribs over the base dome.
  - Outpost: five barracks domes around the hub, each joined to it, plus lamps.
  - Town: four glass greenhouse pods with plants and a grow light, set between the barracks and joined to them.
  - City: two more domes, joined to the barracks:
    - a glass entertainment dome with color-cycling light rings and a spinning hologram on top
    - a management dome with a glass command cupola, a turning dish and a beacon
  - With a settlement, the close-up camera aims 60% of the way from the base toward the settlement.
- **Balance:** settlements made the simulator about 10% faster even at high prices, because the level 3 perks stack. So base output went from 3.55 to 3.9 (see below) and pacing is back to about the same (warp 1 at 3.3 h, warp 5 at 19.2 h).
## Output and hauling
- Base ore per second for planet i (Cinder is 0.8): claim price^0.7 ÷ (2.9 × average ore value). Later planets pay back more slowly, which is what makes warping worthwhile.
  - The divisor was 3 before space probes.
  - It went to 3.9 so probes didn't speed up progress.
  - It went to 3.55 when research started costing ore, to keep the same overall pace.
  - It went to 3.9 when settlements were added, to keep the same overall pace.
  - It went to 3.4 when Ship speed and Cargo were made different, to keep the same overall pace.
  - It went to 2.9 when handling became a fixed, slower 28 s, to keep the same overall pace.
- **Hauler trips:**
  - The hauler cruises at 0.8 AU/s at base speed.
  - Each round trip also includes 28 seconds of fixed steps that speed never shortens: undock 5, orbit in 4, descend 3, load on the pad 4, lift off 3, orbit out 4 and dock 5.
  - The ship is berthed for 70% of each dock and undock step, about 4.2 s in total at base speed.
  - Trip time is (2 × orbit radius + 12.8) ÷ ship speed. Cinder's base trip is 28.5 s.
  - Ship speed upgrades shorten the whole trip, these steps included.
  - Ore is loaded at the exact middle of the pause on the pad.
  - Ore is delivered at the middle of the unloading stop at the station.
- **Docking ports:**
  - One port per claimed planet (at least 4, up to 24), spread evenly round the habitat ring and re-spaced whenever one is added. Planet i uses port i.
  - Each port is a box that passes right through the ring tube, sticking out equally inside and outside. It has a docking collar and hatch, and hinged yellow clamp arms that swing shut while a ship is berthed and open when it leaves.
  - Each port has three green lights (one on top, two on the docking face). They're lit only while a ship is berthed.
  - The ring's outer face has the same even number of lit windows between every pair of ports (4 per gap up to 6 ports, then 2).
  - Ships swing around the station at a safe radius (2.75) to reach their port, berth nose-in, turn with the ring while unloading, then back out. Ships shrink to 0.45 scale for docking and landing.
- **Overflow:**
  - The colony stockpile holds one cargo load, which keeps ships leaving full whenever mining outpaces them. Mined ore beyond it goes straight to an overflow store, so any surplus shows up there within the first trip. It used to hold 5 minutes of mining plus a load, and anything beyond that was lost.
  - When ships have spare capacity (cargo per trip time above the mine rate), the stockpile tops up from the overflow, so spare ships carry it off at full price. This works online and offline. The Planet tab then reads 'Ships are keeping up · carrying off the overflow', and the 25% sale timer shows whenever any overflow is waiting. Pacing is unchanged (warp 5 at 18.6 h, all 22 planets at about 95 h).
  - Every 10 minutes (`OVER_EVERY`), all overflow is sold at 25% of normal price (`OVER_PRICE`, a 75% discount), with a message.
  - While the game is closed, surplus mining (mine rate minus hauling, × time away) is sold the same way and counts in the welcome-back total.
  - Pacing barely changes (warp 5 at 18.9 h vs 19.2 h), because a well-balanced planet has little overflow.
- **Showing the waste:**
  - When ships can't keep up, the Planet tab shows a red line: the share of mined ore that can't be hauled, the overflow waiting and the time to the next sale.
  - The Mining row in the team card says the upgrade won't raise income until the ships catch up.
- **Ship speed vs Cargo:**
  - Every speed boost only speeds up the flight between station and planet: team Ship speed points, research, event boosts, and settlement upgrades and perks.
  - Docking, orbiting, landing and loading always take 28 s (`SHIP_OVH`):
    - undock 5, orbit in 4, descend 3, load 4, ascend 3, orbit out 4, dock 5 (`HANDLE_SEG`, `DOCK_T`)
    - slowed from 16 s on purpose, so it looks controlled
  - Each team Cargo point adds hold space (+0.25×) but makes the ship heavier: flight speed ÷ (1 + 0.06 × Cargo points) (`CARGO_MASS`).
  - Trip time = flight (2 × orbit radius ÷ flight speed) + 28 s handling. The Ship speed row shows both parts.
  - Near planets favor Cargo and far planets favor Ship speed. For the first point:
    - Cinder: Cargo +22.7% vs Ship +6.6%
    - Nyx-4: Cargo +19.7% vs Ship +17.2%
    - Calder outward, Ship speed wins (Zenith: Ship +21.2% vs Cargo +18.8%)
  - As Cargo points pile up their weight costs more, so players end up mixing both.
  - When ships are the bottleneck, only the better of the two gets the Bottleneck tag and red button (`bestHaulTrait`). Team rows say "speeds up the flight only" or "heavier ship: −6% flight speed per point". The Mining, Ship speed and Cargo panes all share one layout and height: the name, a value line (+x× · n points) and a two-line note. The Mining note reads "more ore mined per second", or in red "won't raise income until ships catch up" when ships are behind.
  - No layout jumps: the Planet tab reserves fixed space for everything whose wording changes, so the cards below never move. **Beta 0.18.10.108 audit (every tab, 360 and 412 px):** the probe is one card with the same rows in or out (title and time, bar, status line, Expected haul, Worth about), and its heading keeps the zoom control's shape even when docked; text that can run to two lines keeps room for two (`.l2`: Station orbit text, the Version card's status; the tuning chart status line now sits on its own line with two lines reserved); the parked planet's Station orbit card has a disabled 'Parked here' button like the others' Park button; mission tags stay on one row (running tag shortened to 'x left', ellipsis as a guard); all buttons have a 1 px border (transparent on filled ones) so filled and outlined buttons are the same height; the Version card's messages were shortened to fit two lines. Remaining size differences are deliberate layout changes, not messages: a mission swaps its Accept button for a progress bar when accepted, and settlement text differs between planets. The audit script is `pwaudit.py`. Mine rate and Cargo hold get 2 lines, Ship speed 3. The overflow note is always shown: red when ships are behind, green 'Ships are keeping up' otherwise. The free-points line always shows, reading 'No free points to place' when there are none.
  - Ships' on-screen timelines follow the current flight time (`layFor`).
  - **Pacing:** the base output divisor went to 2.9.
    - First run: 8th planet at 2.6 h, warp 1 at 3.2 h, warp 5 at 19.1 h.
    - All 22 planets at about 95 h of nonstop play (the previous version: 93 h).
- **How upgrades turn into income:** income is the lower of mine rate and hauling, where hauling is cargo ÷ trip time. Ship speed and Cargo multiply each other, so balanced planets end up with more Mining points than Ship or Cargo points. Probes use the mine rate, so surplus mining still earns about 30% through probes.
- **Orbit transitions:**
  - The flight curves into and out of orbit along the orbit's direction of travel (quadratic Bezier, `bezIn` / `bezOut`), instead of meeting it at a sharp angle.
  - The flight eases in and out (smoothstep), and so do the orbit-in, orbit-out, descent and ascent segments. Speed changes where the flight meets the orbit are 25–50× smaller than before.
  - The arc of each flight eases at both ends (sin²).
  - Orbit-in and descent are one curve: the ship starts lowering while still finishing its turn round the planet (angle eases over the first 62% of the combined time, height over the last 62%). The final drop onto the pad is still vertical.
  - The climb and orbit-out are blended the same way, so the ship starts swinging round before the climb ends.
  - Ships keep moving at about a third of top speed through these points, where before they stopped dead and changed direction.
  - Ships turn smoothly towards their new heading (slerp, rate 5/s, 12/s while docked) instead of snapping. For example, a ship tips nose-up gradually as its descent begins.
- **Landing struts:**
  - Every hauler has four hinged struts around its base, each with a foot pad and an amber lamp (shared glow).
  - Struts are 0.17 long, hinged just inside the hull near the base. In flight they fold up and slightly inward (`STRUT_UP`) so the whole strut, foot included, sits inside the six-sided hull. That's checked against the hull's flat faces. They swing down and out over the middle of the descent (35–80%) and are fully locked before touchdown.
  - The ship stands on them on the pad, its hull held about 0.08 clear of the pad surface.
  - They fold away during the climb (30–70% of the ascent), once the ship is clear of the pad.
- **Ship effects:**
  - While backing out of the dock, bright blue-white reverse thrusters fire at the nose (0.95 opacity, up to 0.34 size) and the main engine is off.
  - The main engine burns harder as the ship descends to the pad (throttle 0.8 → 1.6 at touchdown) and hardest on lift-off (1.7, easing to 0.8 as it climbs).
  - Pale dust and smoke billow across the launch pad, tinted slightly to the planet's second color (70% toward off-white so it shows on any ground), during the last half of the descent and the first half of the climb. Strongest at touchdown and lift-off; 18 puffs per pad, up to 0.9 opacity.
- **Cargo:** base cargo is sized to the whole round trip, so base hauling is about 90% of base mining. Changing ship timing doesn't change earnings.
- **First delivery:** in a new game, and after each warp, Cinder's hauler starts already loaded and homeward. The first delivery arrives in about 10 seconds.
- Research multiplies mining, ship speed, cargo, sale value and smelting across all planets.
## Research costs

- **Ore tips:** in research cards, each ore and bar name is dotted-underlined. Hovering it with a mouse, tapping it (shown for 3.5 s) or focusing it with the keyboard shows which planets mine it, with claimed planets highlighted and unclaimed ones marked 'not claimed yet'. Bars say to smelt that ore and where the ore is mined. Only scanned planets (claimed ones plus the next one waiting to be claimed, i.e. those shown on the map) are named. An ore counts as discovered once the first planet that has it has been scanned. Until then, research cards show it as 'Not discovered' (or 'Not discovered bars'), with a dashed empty swatch, and its tip says 'Keep claiming planets to discover it.' The Smelter's Research needs rows use the same label.
- **Completed projects:** listed under Completed as green-bordered cards, styled like completed event missions. Each shows the name (no tag; the green border marks it done), the description and a 'Paid:' line (credits, ore and bars spent).
- **Price:** each project costs 60% of its original credit price plus ore worth about 30% of it.
- **Number of ore types:**
  - 1 type under 10K credits
  - 2 types under 100M
  - 3 types from 100M up
- **Which ores:** the newest ore types mined by planets costing up to 2/3 of the project's original price, so you're already mining them. The amount is split 60/40 or 50/35/15, newest ore first.
- **Bars (projects of 100M credits and up, 12 projects from Core Siphons on):**
  - They also need bars of their second ore type, for example 190 Sunpearl bars for Core Siphons or 190 Abyssium bars for Galactic Reserve.
  - The amount is about 8 minutes of smelting on three furnaces with Plasma Furnaces (and Star Forges for projects priced above it), so 130–260 bars, using only about 1,000–2,000 ore.
  - Research cards show a bars row, and the Smelter tab lists what research needs. Sell everything and auto-sell keep those bars back.
  - Pacing is unchanged in the simulator (Core Siphons at 2.90 h vs 2.88 h).
- **Holding ore back:** Sell everything and auto-sell hold back, for each ore, the most any open project needs. Furnaces never use that held-back ore (`smeltAvail`, Beta 0.17.4.96), and auto-sell keeps the furnaces' working buffer (2 bars' worth per furnace on that ore) on top of it, so a project's ore count holds steady instead of dipping each time a bar starts. Before, the furnace ate into the reserve and the count flickered (e.g. 139 K / 140 K for Slipstream Lanes). A furnace left with only reserved ore shows 'Ore held for research'. Offline smelting follows the same rule. The individual Sell button sells all of that ore. Market rows show how much is kept for research.
- **Research cards:** each card shows have/need bars for credits and each ore, green when covered and red when short.
## Smelter tab

- **Smelting as a flow (Beta 0.18.0.98):** each furnace works at up to `smeltCap` = 8 ÷ smelt time ore per second, drawing continuously from stock (`sl.p` is the share of the next bar already in the furnace; switching ore returns it). Auto-sell keeps `reserve(o)` = `SMELT_BUF` (90 s) of the furnaces' full demand on that ore on top of research's reserve, which bridges deliveries, so an under-fed furnace just runs slower instead of stopping and starting. Offline uses the same rule. Old saves: a furnace mid-bar gets back the ore not yet smelted.
- **Held ore:** furnaces don't use ore open research is waiting for (`smeltAvail`), except an ore whose bars research is also waiting for (`barNeeds`): those bars come first. Without that exception Core Siphons' 190 Sunpearl bars waited for 130 K Sunpearl ore held for a much later project, and the simulator's all-planets time went from 95 h to 99 h.
- **Model (`smeltModel`, `smeltRates`):** per discovered ore, supply = Σ claimed planets' average hauled × ore yield × mix share (0 while the stock is still below research's reserve, unless its bars are needed), plus stock above research needs over 10 minutes; gain per ore = bar price ÷ 8 − ore price. n furnaces turn min(supply, n × capacity) a second into bars. **Furnace cards (Beta 0.18.6.104)** keep one fixed layout in every state, so picking an ore never moves the page: bars/min at the top right, the progress bar, then one status line (`.fstat`, one line, 18 px) in the form 'State · detail': 'Full speed · 100% busy' (green), 'Slowed · 63% busy, not enough Rustcore arriving', 'Paused · Duskquartz held for research (139 K / 140 K)', 'Paused · no Duskquartz arriving yet' (orange), or 'Off · pick an ore below to start smelting'. The tab shows the static '8 ore → 1 bar worth 12× one ore' and a fixed one-line row 'Smelting adds, on top of selling the ore … x/s'.
- **Balance (`smeltBalance`, window `#sm`):** optionally research first (each ore research needs bars of and that's arriving gets one furnace, fastest-to-fill first), then furnaces go one at a time to the ore adding the most credits a second (the value per ore is concave in furnaces, so greedy is optimal); furnaces keep their ore where possible; one with nothing worth smelting goes Off. The window lists each furnace's change, smelting income now → after, and research bars with their ready time; 'Research bars first' is remembered (`S.smRes`).
- **Dot:** only for a furnace switched off, a furnace whose ore has stopped arriving, Balance adding 10%+ (or giving a research ore its first furnace), or a new ore. Normal gaps between deliveries no longer flash it.
- **Pacing:** simulator warp 5 at 19.1 h (19.2 h with parking), all 22 planets at 95.5 h (95.6 h).
- Each furnace card has the Off button next to its name (a toggle: solid orange while the furnace is off), and its ore row lists only ores.
- Opening the Smelter tab centres each furnace's ore row on the ore it's smelting.
## Space probes
- **Schedule:**
  - A probe launches from the station's iris door every 5 minutes.
  - The first launches 60 seconds after a new game or warp.
  - Each flight lasts 3 minutes, so only one probe is out at a time.
- **Orientation:** while rising out of and dropping into the iris, the probe holds still, pointing straight up. It eases into its flight direction over the next 5% of the flight, and back to upright over the 5% before landing. Its size eases the same way, from 0.55 at the station to flight size (bigger with distance, 1.1 when the camera follows it).
- **Flight:** it rises out of the iris, loops out across the claimed part of the galaxy and back, then drops into the iris. The iris opens as it passes, and the probe glows in the color of the ore it's after.
- **Model** (forward is +z):
  - a six-sided gold-foil body with trim bands and a dark instrument deck
  - a high-gain dish on a tall mast, clear of the deck, turning slowly (0.5 rad/s)
  - a front collector scoop with a spinning intake ring in the target ore's color
  - two solar wings with cell textures on hinged booms, slowly turning to track the sun
  - a finned power unit underneath, a long sensor boom, and a scanner lens pulsing in the ore color
  - three rear thrusters with an engine flame while cruising (off during launch and landing)
  - red and green wingtip lights and a blinking beacon, all with the shared glow
- **Haul:**
  - Each probe brings back one random ore type you have discovered.
  - The amount is worth 90 seconds of the combined mining of every claimed planet, ±15%.
  - It's based on mine rate, not hauling, so it helps most when ships are the bottleneck.
  - It goes into the Market, and auto-sell applies.
- **Time away:** probes run on the real clock, so they keep flying while the game is closed, up to the offline earnings limit (8 h, or 24 h with Night Shift). The welcome-back message counts them.
- **Display:**
  - The top bar shows "Next probe in …" or "Probe out · back in …".
  - Tapping it zooms to the probe (level 1), or to the station if no probe is out.
  - The Probe tab shows the current flight and expected haul, totals for this galaxy and all galaxies, ore collected, and recent returns.
## Weekly events

- **Beta 0.19.0.111 overhaul (supersedes the goal list and the one-a-week rule below):**
  - **Pace-sized goals:** every target comes from the player's own rates when accepting (`evTarget`), so nothing can be finished at once with banked credits. Rate goals use 18/24/30 h of the rate for 3/4/5-day missions (counting offline): earn credits (income), haul newest ore and second-newest ore (`evOreRate`, average hauled × yield × mix), deliveries (×0.6), probe hauls by value (×0.6, `evProbeValRate`), ore hauled by value (×0.8), bars and bar value smelted (×0.6, from the smelter model; Balance's plan if the furnaces are off). Play goals: keep every planet keeping up (no red names) and keep every furnace working, 2/3/4 h of play (`S.life.keepS`, `busyS`, ticked by `evPlayTick` while the game is open); Market Surge bonus credits ≈ 3/4/5 surges × 10 min of income × 0.7; raise income by 15/20/25% (progress = today's income from its value at acceptance). Kinds: credits, ore, deliv, probes, keep, ore2, grow, bars, surge, busy, barval, haulval (replacing ups, teams, settle, od, warp, claim, research, which still work for missions already accepted). Each card shows its pace ('about 15h at your current pace', '3h of play', 'about 4 Market Surges', '+20% income, as you upgrade').
  - **Three a week, one at a time:** `E.taken[w]` counts this week's (old saves: true = 1), `E.used[w]` the slots, `E.res[w]` their results, `E.wins[w]` completions. After two completions the remaining mission is **Elite** (`evElite`): 1.5× goal, reward +2% boost, double star dust, +1 research project or double points; amber border and 'Elite' tag, 'Accept Elite mission'. Unavailable reasons: one running, a reward to claim, or all three taken.
  - **Streak:** consecutive weeks with a completion (`E.streak`, `E.lastWin`); the first completion of each week adds min(streak, 5) star dust from a 2-week streak. Shown with 'Missions this week x / 3'.
  - **Galaxy events (GALAXY EVENTS section):** one per 12-hour window from the missions' anchor, starting at a seeded time in its first 6 hours (`galDef`, same for every player, no server). Ore rush (4 h): one discovered ore and its bars sell for 1.5× (`galRush` in `itemValue`; 2× made the simulator 3% faster). Solar storm (4 h): flight speed ×0.8 (`galStorm` in `shipG`). Comet pass (3 h): probe hauls ×2 (`galComet`), with a comet and glowing tail crossing the map. Derelict freighter (6 h): a tumbling wreck with an amber blinking light and halo beside a claimed planet; tap it to salvage 10 minutes of income; it's in `FOCUS_OBJ` so 'Show me the derelict' zooms to it. Each has a challenge tracked from when it's first seen (`galChallenge`): haul a rate-sized amount of the rush ore; keep every planet keeping up for 30 min of play during the storm; probe hauls worth a rate-sized amount during the comet; salvage the derelict. Reward: 15 minutes of income. A Galaxy event card at the top of the Events tab keeps one size in every state (effect, goal, bar, progress, button); a toast announces each event; the Events dot shows news. `galNow` is worked out at most once a second.
  - **Pacing:** simulator all 22 planets at 94.2 h with events (94.8 h without; the bot doesn't claim mission or challenge rewards).
- **Schedule:** three new missions every Friday at 18:00, phone local time. The current week runs from the last Friday 18:00, numbered from Friday 2 January 2026, and is correct across daylight-saving changes. Everything is worked out from the date, with no server, so every player gets the same missions that week.
- **Choosing:** accept one mission per week. Another can't be accepted while one is active or waiting to be claimed. A mission has 3, 4 or 5 days from acceptance; if time runs out it fails, with no penalty.
- **No repeats:** 12 mission types × 13 story settings = 156 events, laid out over a 52-week cycle (`evDef`).
  - Each event appears once a year, and the smallest gap between repeats is exactly 52 weeks.
  - Each week's three always differ in type and setting.
  - Durations are evenly split, 52 each of 3, 4 and 5 days. Tested over 620 weeks.
- **Mission types:**
  - Goals sized to the player's progress when accepting, using 18, 24 or 30 hours for 3, 4 or 5 days: earn credits (current income), haul your newest ore, and hauler deliveries (60% of the current delivery rate).
  - Fixed goals (3, 4, 5 days):
    - probes returned: 30 / 40 / 50
    - team upgrades: 20 / 28 / 36
    - teams hired: 2 / 3 / 4
    - settlement levels: 1 / 2 / 2
    - bars smelted: 150 / 200 / 260
    - Market Surges called: 6 / 8 / 10
    - warp once
    - planets claimed: 1 / 2 / 2
    - research projects: 2 / 3 / 4
  - Cards show requirements: the Smelter for bars, and the warp unlock planet for warping.
- **Stories:** the setting's group and situation, a bridging phrase, then what they need. For example: "The Tidejade Lagoon Council is rebuilding its sea walls after a tidal surge. To pull it off, they need answers only research can give."
- **Rewards** (each week's three choices give different kinds):
  - Slot 1: a permanent boost of +3/4/5% to mining, ship speed, cargo, sale prices, probe hauls or smelting speed, rotating weekly. Stored in `S.perm` and kept through warps.
  - Slot 2: star dust, 8/11/15 plus 3% of current dust.
  - Slot 3, in the current galaxy, alternating weekly: free research (1, or 2 for 5 days; the cheapest open projects) or free team upgrade points (8/12/16, placed with the green buttons). Falls back to star dust if nothing can be unlocked.
- **Progress** is measured from lifetime counters in `S.life` (ore delivered by type, deliveries, bars, upgrades, teams, claims, settlement levels, research, Market Surges), plus lifetime credits, probe returns and warps. All are kept through warps and counted offline too.
- **Interface:** an Events tab, between Probe and Warp, with a dot when new missions haven't been seen or a reward is ready to claim. It shows the current mission (story, goal, progress bar, time left, claim button), this week's three offers, a countdown to the next Friday, and permanent event boosts earned.
- The device clock decides the week, so changing the phone's clock could skip ahead. That's acceptable for a game with no purchases.
- **One card per mission:** status, progress and buttons all live on the mission's own card in this week's list. No separate status card is added. The accepted mission shows 'In progress · time left', a progress bar and its count. When finished it gets a green border, a ✓ Completed badge and a Claim reward button, which then reads 'Reward claimed'. A failed mission shows ✕ Failed. The missions you didn't pick show an Unavailable button with an (i) icon inside it; tapping it explains why: one mission per week (naming the one you chose, with new missions on Friday at 6 pm), last week's mission still running (with time left), or last week's reward not yet claimed. The only extra card is 'Last week's mission', which appears only when a mission accepted late last week is still running or unclaimed.
- **Permanent boost sources:** each row in Permanent event boosts has an (i) button. Tapping it shows the completed mission card or cards that gave that boost (title, completion date, story, goal, full bar, reward). Claims save a snapshot in `S.ev.src`. Older saves are rebuilt once from the claim log by matching past permanent-boost mission titles; those cards have no goal line.
- **Status tag placement:** the status tag (In progress · time left, ✓ Completed, ✕ Failed) sits in the same row as the days and reward-type tags, at the same size, coloured teal, green or red. On boost-source cards the completion date sits next to the count as 'Finished Oct 22'.
## Market Surge (replaced Overdrive in Beta 1.0.45)

- Tap to call a Market Surge: for 10 minutes everything sold (ore and bars, by hand or auto-sell) earns double.
- The extra is capped by a bonus pool worth 10 minutes of current income, set when the surge is called. Stockpiling and dumping during a surge earns no more than steady selling. If the pool runs dry the surge ends early.
- It then recharges for 60 minutes, counted from when the surge ends.
- Its status now lives in the Market tab's Market Surge card ('Ready · 2× for 10 minutes', 'Surge 2× · time left · bonus left', 'Recharging · ready in …'), with a ▲ icon under the credits that lights up when it's ready (Beta 0.18.7.105).
- Mining and ship speed are untouched, so it never causes bottlenecks (Overdrive's 2x mining outran hauling). Used every time it's ready, it's worth about +14% average income.
- Missions that asked to fire Overdrive now ask for Market Surges (same counts). Saves keep the old internal state, so existing games carry over.
## Scrolling lists

- **Sideways lists (Beta 1.0.69):** tapping an item that's partly hidden in any sideways-scrolling list (tabs, planets, crew picker, furnace ores) glides the list until it's fully in view, clear of the edge fade on a side with more items (`revealLeft`). The panel may redraw on the same tap, so the item is found again in the redrawn list, and checked once more after 450 ms in case a redraw cut the glide short. If the list already scrolled it into view itself (picking a planet centres it), nothing more happens.
- **Smooth centring (Beta 0.18.5.103):** tapping an item in any sideways list that scrolls (planets, tabs, crews, furnace ores) glides it to the middle of the list, as far as the ends allow, replacing the old 'just bring it into view' rule. `hGlide` eases the scroll over 320 ms (cubic ease-out) and finds the list again each frame by its key (`listKey`: its id, the tab row, or its place among the panel's lists), since the panel redraws often and keeps scroll positions. Touching, dragging or wheel-scrolling a list stops its glide. A change of selected planet glides the planet list; opening the Planet tab still jumps straight to the selected planet; switching tabs glides the tab row.
- **Selected planet stays in view (Beta 1.0.70):** on the Planet tab, whenever the selected planet's chip is partly out of sight (switching to the tab, opening the panel, a resize or turn, any redraw), the list centres it again (`keepSelChip`), unless the player touched the list in the last 4 seconds (`pchipsTouched`). Switching tabs no longer reuses another tab's list scroll positions (`renderedTab`).
- **User guide:** opening a section that runs off the bottom of the guide scrolls it up, as far as keeping its title on screen.

## Code organization and performance

## Galaxy Warp and star dust
- Unlocks once Kallyur'Dadh (planet 6) is claimed.

## Planet surfaces
- **Textures:** 512×256, generated in the game from seeded 3D noise sampled over the sphere, so there are no seams or pinched poles. Each planet's look is fixed by its index.
- **Rocky planets:**
  - warped terrain shading from dark lowlands through the planet's two colors to pale peaks
  - 6–21 craters with raised rims
  - fine surface detail
  - ice caps above about 60° latitude on icy worlds
  - a bump map from the terrain height
- **Gas giants:** turbulent bands, fine streaks and one oval storm.
- **Atmosphere:** every planet has a soft edge glow, dimmer on unclaimed planets.

## Sun and station orbit

- **Sun:** a star at the galaxy's centre (radius 2.6), modelled on an ultraviolet photo of the real sun and drawn by shaders from 3D noise (no seams):
  - **Surface:** a mottled deep-orange disc threaded with soft dark filaments, and bright yellow active regions laced with glowing loops.
  - **Sunspots:** dark umbras with penumbras that drift as the sun turns and slowly grow and shrink. The sun turns as one body; an earlier faster-equator rotation twisted the surface into swirls over time.
  - **Rim:** a bright orange rim.
  - **Flares:** a camera-facing sheet draws a hot rim glow, a ragged fringe of spicules all the way round, and five big flare tongues that drift round the edge, flare up and die back.
  - **Glow:** a red-orange core glow, a breathing halo and a faint corona.
  - **Light source:** a point light at the centre lights every planet and the station, so planets have a sunlit side and a night side.
- **Orbit spacing:** `ORBIT_R` stays the gameplay distance (`P.er`: trip times, base cargo, the AU shown in the Planet tab). The drawn orbits (`P.r`) start with Cinder at 9, well clear of the sun. Each later orbit sits outward from the one before by the two planets' bands plus a 2.4 gap. A planet's band is its rings (2.1 × size) or the circle its haulers fly round it (2 × size), whichever is wider, plus 0.3. The drawn orbits run from 9 (Cinder) to about 171 (Zenith).
- **Station position:** the station has no lane of its own. Every galaxy starts with it parked above Cinder, 4.2 units up and 3.2 ahead along Cinder's orbit, moving with it. Station Thrusters move it to another claimed planet; there's no return to a lane. Old saves are migrated to parked at Cinder.
- **Real orbits:** every body's period is 1.18 × r^1.5 minutes (`orbitW`), so inner planets go round faster: Cinder about 32 min, the outermost about 44 h. Positions come from the clock (seconds since 1 Jan 2026), so they're the same after a reload or time away and can be forecast. A planet's cycle round the station (its synodic period) runs from 29 min (Cinder) to about 2.7 h (Orra); most outer planets take about an hour.
- **Distance and trips:** the gap between each planet and the station grows and shrinks as they go round at different speeds. Flight time follows it, softened and measured against the planet's average gap with the station parked at Cinder (`distF = 1 + 0.7 × (gap / that average − 1)`, at least 0.35). So a new galaxy plays exactly as the gameplay distances say, and moving the station changes every planet's average. Handling (docking, landing, loading) stays fixed.
- **Averages for planning:** hauling shown in the UI, the red best-upgrade button, income estimates and time away all use capacity averaged over a whole cycle (`capAvg`, `haul`). The live game uses the moment-to-moment trip time.
- **Ore silos:** the colony stockpile is a silo holding 1 cargo load, 3 with Ore Silos research (30 K, after Expanded Holds) and 8 with Deep Silos (8 M). In the far stretch the silo fills; at the close pass the ships' spare capacity empties it at full price. Anything beyond the silo goes to the overflow (25% sale). `siloNeed` runs the balance over two cycles to say whether the silo is big enough.
- **Cycle chart (Beta 1.0.60: part of the tuning chart in the Mining crews card):** Mined and Hauled avg at the top, then hauling capacity over the next whole cycle (now at the left, a dashed marker at the next close pass) against the mining line, teal where the silo empties and red where it fills. Below it:
  - a silo gauge (stock / capacity)
  - a status line: green when ships keep up over the cycle, amber "Silo too small for the far stretch · about N overflows each cycle", or red when the average itself is short
  - 'Close pass in …' with the overflow and its sale timer
- **Speed row:** shows the trip range (e.g. 'trip 36–44s'). 'Next:' previews the average trip and the average capacity.

## Background sky (Beta 1.0.82)

## Visual rules for new graphics
- **Glow on every light:** any light element (a mesh whose material is emissive at intensity 1 or more) gets a glow from `glowify(group, list)`, run once after building. `updateGlows(list)` then runs each frame so the glow follows the light's color and brightness.
  - Small spheres (lamps, beacons, grow lights) get a soft sprite halo, 7× their radius, at 0.8 opacity.
  - Rings (torus) get an additive shell 3.2× thicker, at 0.3 opacity.
  - Lit bands (open cylinders) get an additive shell 1.07× wider and 2.4× taller, at 0.28 opacity.
  - Glass and greenhouse domes (emissive under 1) don't glow.
  - Small box lights (ring windows, conveyor pods) get a sprite 2.6× their longest side, at 0.6 opacity. Long thin strips, like hall windows, are skipped.
  - Lights that start dark, like dock lights and the iris rim, are marked with `material.userData.light` so they get a glow that switches on with them.
  - Textured hulls with lit portholes (an emissive map) aren't treated as lights.
- **Where it's applied:** settlements, colony bases and launch pads, and the space station: dock lights, ring windows, core window bands and the iris rim. The station's blinking navigation lights keep their own glow; the probe's lights use the shared glow. Use the same helper for anything new.
- Code comments go on their own line, never after code on the same line. Comments placed after code have cut off code three times.

- **Version comparison (`verKey`, `verGT`):** compares the build number only (the 4th part; an old 'Beta 1.0.N' name counts as build N, anything else as 0).
- **Version card (Warp tab):** the version, the last check result ('Up to date · checked 5m ago', 'Beta x is available. It installs next time you open the game.', or a failure), a Check for updates button (Update to Beta x once one is found; About this update in the Android app), What's changed, and a note that the game is in beta, updates come out once a day by 8 am Central and install themselves the next time the game is opened, and progress carries over.
- **Change log:** What's changed opens a scrollable list grouped by day, newest first. Each item shows its version tag, when it has one, and the change. It was seeded from the git history (108 entries from 20 Oct); entries from before build numbers (20–23 Oct) have dates only.
- **Update check:** the game fetches `version.json` (no-store; the service worker never caches it) 20 s after loading, every 30 minutes, and on returning to the foreground if 5+ minutes have passed. Sources are tried in order, each with an 8 s timeout: the repository's raw file (`raw.githubusercontent.com/namewastaken-creator/star-quarry/main/version.json`, which allows cross-site requests and is cached up to 5 minutes, so a push to `main` is noticed without waiting for the Pages deploy), then the GitHub API copy (`api.github.com/.../contents/version.json`, raw), then the Pages site.
- **Quiet updates:** when an automatic check finds a higher `n`, the web game doesn't ask. It notes the version being played (`sq_updfrom`), and the next launch loads the new version (the service worker always fetches the page fresh) and toasts 'Updated to Beta x. Your progress came with you.'
- **When the game asks:** an automatic check shows the Update available dialog only if a change newer than the player's version is marked important, the player hasn't said Not now since loading, and it last asked over a day ago (`sq_updask`). Checking by hand always shows it.
  - The dialog says 'A new version is ready. You're playing Beta x.', then 'What's new:' with up to six of the newer changes ('· and N more in What's changed'), or the newest version's `notes`.
  - **Update now** saves, notes `sq_updfrom` and the expected build (`sq_updto`), and reloads. If the page that loads is still older than that build (Pages can be a minute or two behind the repository), the game says the new version is still being published and waits 3 minutes before checking again.
  - **Not now** toasts that the player keeps this version, won't be asked again until the game is reloaded, and can update any time from the Warp tab. Automatic prompts then stop until reload.
  - **Android app:** no quiet update. The dialog explains that the app updates when its newest build is installed, and offers only OK.
- **Failed checks:** if every source fails, the game stays silent unless the player tapped Check. On GitHub Pages, in the Android app or on localhost the message is 'Couldn't reach GitHub. Check your internet connection and try again.' Anywhere else, such as the Claude artifact, whose page blocks outside connections, it explains that this page blocks the check and points to namewastaken-creator.github.io/star-quarry.
- **Erase save panel:** the last panel on the Warp tab, below Your save (Beta 0.41.0.194, see "Later changes"), laid out like the Version panel: a heading, a one-line summary, the 'Erase save and start over' button (tap twice within 4 s), and a note on what's erased (everything except the music setting), that it can't be undone, and that Warp keeps star dust and boosts.

- **Quiet updates:** an open game that finds a newer version doesn't ask; it notes the old version (`sq_updfrom`), and the next launch loads the new one (the service worker always fetches the page fresh) and says 'Updated to …'. - **Repository handoff (30 Oct 2026, at Beta 0.40.9.191, no version bump).** The pipeline that lived in the owning chat's workspace moved into the repository: `src/game.html`, `src/changelog.json`, `src/guide_state.json`, `src/vendor/three-r128.min.js`; `src/bump.py` (the only bump script; the reference-only `tools/bump.py` is gone), `src/build.py`, `src/guidecheck.py`, `src/artifact/mk.py` and `preview.html`; `android/` (without signing material or `local.properties`); `tools/check.py` with the browser tests in `tools/tests/` (ten then) and the visual check tools; `BUILDING.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and a `.gitignore`. The chat that built and pushed the game until then can still read the project and suggest changes, but no longer builds or pushes.
- **Parking ranking, no freeze on tap (Beta 0.40.10.192, important).** The Park window never works out a forecast on the spot any more (tapping Park while the ranking was re-running did, which froze phones for about a second late in the game). It shows "Working out the forecast…", the running ranking does that spot next (`PR_WANT`, `parkFor`, `parkAskRefresh`), and the window fills itself in. `planBalance` scores each layout once (`memo`), about a third less work with identical results. A ranking's age is wall-clock time (`Date.now`) and one older than `PR_REFRESH` no longer counts as ready (a phone's performance clock stops while it sleeps). The Best parking spot card no longer offers the spot the station is already parked at while it re-ranks. Test: `tools/tests/pwframe.py` with the maxed save `tools/tests/lategame.json` (made by `mklategame.py`).
- **Offline copy (Beta 0.40.11.193).** The service worker keeps only the game's own address (the folder and `index.html`) for offline play, and replaces that copy only with a 200 for that address that isn't a redirect, is HTML and contains the game (`realGame`, marker `starquarry_save_v1`). A Wi-Fi sign-in page, a server error, a redirect or no network gets the offline copy instead. Other pages on the site are left to the network. Other files are cached only on a confirmed success. Test: `tools/tests/pwsw.py`.
- **Bad saves (Beta 0.40.11.193).** A save that's JSON but the wrong shape (`saveShape`: no planets, a planet that isn't an object, a part of the wrong type) is treated as unreadable; parts an old save simply lacks get empty defaults. Backups never replace each other (`starquarry_save_v1_bak`, then `starquarry_save_v1_bak_<time>`). If the backup can't be written, the game won't save over the original (`SAVE_BLOCK`) and says so. If starting the game with a save fails anyway, nothing is saved and the page reloads once treating that save as unreadable (`BOOT_FAIL`, `BOOTED`). Test: `tools/tests/pwbadsave.py`.
- **Your save: export, import and backups (Beta 0.41.0.194).** A Your save card on the Warp tab (above Erase save) opens the `#sv` window (`svOpen`) in three modes. **Export** shows the game as a sealed save code (`sealSave`): `SQ1-` + an 8-hex nonce + the save (`{v:1,b:build,t:time,s:save}` as UTF-8) scrambled with an xorshift keystream seeded from `SEAL_KEY` and the nonce (`sealStream`), in base64url + a 32-hex checksum keyed with `SEAL_KEY` (`sealMac`, two `cyrb` hashes). Copy code (clipboard, with an `execCommand` fallback) everywhere; Download file (`<station>-<date>.sqsave`) on the web. **Import** takes a pasted code or Load file (web), and `unsealSave` refuses anything that isn't a code, fails the checksum, is from a newer format or fails `saveShape`; nothing changes until Replace my game is tapped twice within 4 s (`SV_ARM`). **Restore a backup** lists every backup (`saveBackups`: `starquarry_save_v1_bak` and `_bak_<time>`, newest first), with Restore (two taps) for those this version can load. Replacing (`replaceSave`) backs up the game being played (`backupSave`, shared with loading; the raw save instead while `SAVE_BLOCK` protects it), refuses if that backup can't be written, writes the new save with `SAVE_BLOCK` on so nothing saves over it, and reloads; the note (`sq_loaded` in session storage) becomes `LOAD_NOTE`. The seal stops casual editing only: the key is in the game, and a save in the browser can be edited in developer tools anyway. The Android app has no file download or picker (its WebView has no download listener or file chooser), so it gets the code only. Test: `tools/tests/pwsavecode.py`.
- **Save migrations run once (not yet released).** The one-time conversions of older saves are an ordered list, `MIGRATIONS`, and `S.mig` counts the steps a save has had, so each runs once (`migrate`, after the crew helpers are defined); a new game or a warp starts with `mig` at the list's length. The five steps, in their old order: crews from before planet levels went (`econ` 2), undoing the 10x star dust award (`dust1`), furnaces from before the smelting flow, removing Handling, and the point pool. Each still checks for the old data itself, so a save from before `S.mig` runs them all and is changed only where it needs to be. A higher `mig` (a save from a newer build) is kept. Filling in parts a save lacks (music, probe, life counters and so on) and resetting a furnace's progress outside 0–1 still happen on every load. `evBackfill` keeps its own flag (`S.ev.srcV`). New conversions go at the end of the list, never reordered or removed. Test: `tools/tests/pwmig.py`.
- **Gas giant bases rise when fully built (not yet released).** The whole gas base (pad, mine head and works, refinery and settlement) floats at one height, `gasLift`: 0.15 s (`GAS_LO`) until every track is at tier 3 (3 crews, pad tier 3 at 24 Speed + Cargo points, all five programs, City), then 0.475 s (`GAS_HI`, half the settlement's old 0.95 s; the rest of the base was at 0.5 s). `syncBase` sets `w.lift`; a change rebuilds every structure (`syncPart` compares it, and the settlement levels are dropped and rebuilt). The pad's height (`w.padH`, where haulers land), the miners' climb onto the works pad (`_padLift`, lift + 0.06 s), the conveyor pods and the refinery's ore slugs all follow it. While low, each platform's keel is made shallower so its thrusters stay above the clouds even as it bobs (`gasRoom` = lift - 0.04 s). Raised, every round platform also gets two pulsing lift rings under its keel (`liftRings`, a pulse running down from the wider ring to the narrower, colour 0x7fe9ff). The settlement keeps its lift beams. The change isn't instant: the base is rebuilt at its new height and then eased there from where it was over `GAS_RISE_T` (4 s, smoothstep), counted only while the base is in view and at most 0.05 s a frame, so the long frame of the rebuild doesn't skip it (`w.riseFrom`, `w.riseK`, `gasRise`). The bobbing platforms and pad add `gasRise`; tubes, transit lights and tube hover nodes follow through `onRise`; conveyor pods, ore slugs and miners add it too. Haulers' touchdown height (`shipPosRel`) adds it as well, so a landing during the rise meets the moving pad. User guide: the gas line in Colonies up close.
